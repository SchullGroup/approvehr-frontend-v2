03An empty company

Every screen is an empty state, and most of them are signposts

This is the moment a product is usually at its worst. Ten screens were walked cold. The good ones state what is missing and offer the one thing that fixes it.

/payroll

No payroll run for August 2026 yet. Running payroll works out everybody's pay and lists anything wrong with their records. It pays nobody, and you can do it as many times as you like.

/settings

Setting up your company — 2 of 6 done. Outstanding: Company profile, Leave types and holidays, Pay setup, Payroll checks.

/people/shifts

No shifts defined yet. Add an early, a late and a night, then build a cycle out of them.

/people/leave

Public holidays 2026 — None on file. Attendance, overtime, payroll proration and the help desk's response clock all read these dates.

The payroll one is the best sentence in the product. The single largest fear a person has about a payroll system they have not used is that pressing the button will pay somebody, and it answers that fear before it is asked.

Worth a product decision

A new Nigerian company gets no public holidays. The calendar starts empty, and the screen says so honestly — but every one of those dates is already load-bearing: payroll proration and the overtime rate read them, and so does the help desk's response clock. Ifeoma has to hand-enter Nigeria's 2026 holidays before Christmas prorates as an ordinary working day.

Not a bug — nothing lies. But Nigeria's public holidays are the same for every customer, and asking each one to type them is asking each one to get them wrong differently.

The dashboard is honest about the same emptiness — On the payroll: 0 — and offers seven starting points, of which two are the ones that matter: Add an employee — one person, on the payroll in a minute, and Import a staff list — a spreadsheet you already keep. She takes the first.

04First employee

"One person, on the payroll in a minute"

A claim on the dashboard is a promise. Five steps: Who they are · Job and pay · Extras (optional) · Review · Done.

Step 1 asks for a first name and a last name and marks everything else optional, with a line above the fields saying so: Fields marked * are required. Everything else can follow later, and the record saves without it. Pressing Continue on an empty step puts First name is required. on the field itself, not in a banner at the top.

Step 3 is where the product does its best work

Three collapsed groups, each labelled Optional, each stating what skipping it costs:

Bank account Optional
Skip this and payroll has nowhere to send the money — you can add the account before the run.

Set up tax Optional
Skip this and their payslip will not show PAYE, and nothing is filed to a state revenue service until you add it.

Pension and NHF Optional
Skip this and no pension is deducted or remitted for them, and the payroll run will hold them back until an RSA PIN is added.

Beside them, a live Payroll readiness panel that separates the one thing that stops pay from the two that do not:

Bank account — They cannot be paid until this is added, or they are excluded from the run.
Pension PIN — Their pension remittance schedule will be incomplete. It does not hold back their pay.
TIN — Recommended for filing their tax return. It does not affect this month's pay.

Ifeoma skips all three. She is adding her loom supervisor at eleven at night and does not have his RSA PIN to hand.

Step 4 says nothing has happened yet

Check this over. Nothing has been saved yet. Anything you skipped can be added to their record afterwards. Each section has its own Edit link back to the step that owns it. Then:

The form is clear and ready for the next person.

Two decisions, not defects

No public holidays for a new company

Nigeria's dates are the same for every customer, and they already drive payroll proration, overtime rates, timesheet status and the SLA clock. Asking every customer to type them is asking every customer to get them wrong differently.

The owner is not on their own payroll

Correct as a data model, and the root of two friction points above. Worth deciding whether signup should offer to create the owner's employee record, or whether the product should say plainly that it has not.

10Honesty

What this walk did not prove

Three things I reported to myself and had to withdraw

Worth recording, because each is a way a test can be wrong in a direction that flatters the tester.

  • "The clock-in fails silently." It does not. My script read main, and the toast renders outside it. Both refused writes show a specific, actionable message.
  • "The record page has no bank-name field." It does. The field is a searchable Picker over 255 NIBSS banks, not a <select>, so my DOM query missed it.
  • "Escape does not close the modal." It does. I had navigated away between opening and pressing.
